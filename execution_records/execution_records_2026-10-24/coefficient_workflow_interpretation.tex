\paragraph{Coefficient changes across workflows (descriptive, post hoc).} Under the
alternative workflow $K_0$ fell by 3.8--4.0\% in every one of the 22 rows (for
example the independent-profile $K_0$ changed by $\mn3.9\%$ [$\mn6.8$,
$\mn0.8$]) and $K_1$ by 3.8--4.0\% in 21 rows, so the ratio, and therefore
$F^*$, was essentially unchanged; the exception, $K_1$ down 10.2\% at
.95$\to$.99, $\rho=0$, $e=1$, is the largest surface change discussed below. At leading order $F^*$
depends on a background mainly through the opportunity-count mix of its
survival-weighted bridges, and that mix was almost identical in the two workflows
(Table~\ref{tab:kmix}). The only leading-order mass that vanished was bridges
with zero intervening opportunities, which were at most 0.3\% of $K$ in the
original workflow. At $p=0$ such a bridge is a \emph{hand} bridge with no
intervening hygiene opportunity, not an environmental route. In the original
workflow some backgrounds carried this zero-opportunity component and none did in
the alternative workflow; correspondingly the per-seed $K_1/K_0$ of the
independent profile had standard deviation 0.0034 in the original workflow
(range .2481--.2786) and 0.00003 in the alternative workflow. The largest surface
change (.95$\to$.99, $\rho=0$, $e=1$: 95.74\%$\to$96.01\%) moved onto the
isolated two-opportunity value of 96.0\%; there $K_1$ is smallest, so the
removed zero-opportunity mass matters most. No background had an identical
ordered bridge-event history in the two workflows (0 of 256), so the isolation
rule changed every history, but only slightly in aggregate. Under the
alternative rules the mean observation window contained 4.92 isolation
relocations, 1.10 detected-patient-days spent waiting in a shared bed, and no
swap; mean observation patient-days were 1,640.4 (original) and 1,640.0
(alternative). Bank A and bank B agreed to within 0.002 percentage points in
every primary limit, in both workflows. "The same backgrounds" therefore means
the same seeds, not the same histories: observation-window imported carriers
averaged 9.20 and 9.53 per background in the two workflows. The
isolation rule moves carriers to different isolation beds, and the bridge
coefficient redistributes among workers accordingly: per-worker mean $K_0$ of
the independent profile changed by up to 2.6-fold between workflows, while the
role shares stayed close (nurses 86.7\% and 86.1\%, physicians 10.6\% and 11.0\%,
radiography 2.7\% and 2.9\% of $K_0$). Because almost every surviving bridge
crosses exactly two opportunities whichever worker forms it, this
redistribution leaves the ratio, and hence $F^*$, essentially unchanged. On this
account, given after the result, the invariance is a property of survival
weighting under the entry/exit rule, not evidence that bed-management rules in general leave the hygiene
response unchanged, and the limit cannot see workflow features that act only
through ungated environmental chains; whatever separates the two workflows'
finite-$p$ fractions lies outside the leading term. The workflow comparison
changes vacancy cleaning and the isolation rule together, so this description
does not isolate either rule's effect.
