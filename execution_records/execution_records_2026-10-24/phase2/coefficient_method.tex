A background $\omega$ is a complete transmission-free ($p=0$) ICU history: 146
days (90 warmup and 56 observation days) with admissions, 3\% imported carriage,
turnover, staff schedules, screening and isolation, clean hands and clean sites,
and the care-zone opportunity rule. Because no transmission occurs, hand
contamination never changes movement, screening or isolation, and the history
is the same in both hygiene arms. For each background and hygiene arm the
engine enumerates every \emph{bridge}: a contact of worker $u$ with an imported
carrier at contact $i$, followed by a contact of the same worker with an
initially susceptible patient at contact $j$ during the observation window,
with no role reset between them. The bridge contributes $c_id_js_h(i,j)$, where
$c_i,d_j\in\{1,.6\}$ are the pickup and transfer weights (the isolation
multiplier applies to isolation locations) and $s_h(i,j)$ is the exact
probability that no intervening opportunity clears the hand. That probability
is the ordered product $\nu_i\prod_m[P(h_m,\rho)\Gamma(e_m)]\boldsymbol 1$ over the
intervening opportunities, started from the actual preceding completion-state
law $\nu_i$ (Section~\ref{sec:s-hygiene}); the policy arm switches $h$ from .90
to .95 at day 90 and keeps completion memory. A pickup in warmup counts when its
transfer falls in the observation window. $K_h(\omega)$ is the sum over bridges;
Proposition~\ref{prop:icu} shows that $\E[K_h]$ is the coefficient of $p^2$ in
expected acquisitions.

The leading-order limit is the ratio of means
$F^*=1-\overline K_{h_1}/\overline K_{h_0}$ over backgrounds (not a mean of
ratios; no background is dropped). $K$ intervals are seed-level normal
approximations; the $F^*$ interval is a normal/delta interval that keeps the
within-background covariance of $K_{h_0}$ and $K_{h_1}$. Paired between-workflow
differences use whole-seed bootstrap resampling (20,000 draws, NumPy seed
20261021, one index matrix shared by every statistic). All 256 backgrounds come
from the original coefficient study's seedbook (banks A and B, 128 each).
Because washing is integrated exactly rather than sampled, the Monte Carlo
intervals reflect only variation between backgrounds; they are narrow because
$K_{h_0}$ and $K_{h_1}$ are computed on the same history and move together.
They do not describe uncertainty about staff behaviour, and the limit provides
no finite-$p$ error bound.
