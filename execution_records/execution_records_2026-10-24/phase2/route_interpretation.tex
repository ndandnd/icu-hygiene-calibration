\paragraph{Route-label results (descriptive).}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Original workflow, independent misses.} Carry-over accounted for 450
of 2,375 control acquisitions (18.9\%) and fell only 29.1\% [17.8, 39.0] under
the policy, whereas the patient, current-stay own-zone and other-zone labels
fell 78.3\%, 75.3\% and 76.7\%. The carry-over share therefore rose to 41.4\% of
policy acquisitions. Excluding carry-over-labelled acquisitions, the count fell
76.6\%; all labels together fell 67.6\%. This is a descriptive count rather than a
counterfactual estimate, because each acquisition also seeds later ones.
\item \emph{Alternative workflow.} Carry-over control counts were 65, 22 and 16
(against 450, 75 and 62 in the original workflow). The current-stay own-zone
share of independent-profile control acquisitions was 39.0\% (38.9\% in the
original workflow) and that label fell 77.6\% under the policy.
\item \emph{Hygiene response of zero-opportunity final steps.} Current-stay own-zone pickups occur almost entirely with
zero intervening opportunities (0--6 per arm otherwise), yet this label fell by
22.4\% to 77.6\%, within 6.3 percentage points of the patient-pickup label in
every profile and workflow (largest gap: original, imperfect clearing), with
overlapping intervals. Such a site can only have been contaminated during the
stay by a hand arriving from elsewhere, and that hand crossed hygiene
opportunities on the way. The carry-over label had the smallest response, which
is consistent with a chain containing no hygiene opportunity at all (for example
a carrier contaminating its own bedside site and the next occupant picking it
up); the labels do not record the donor, so this is not shown directly.
Acquisition from prior room occupants is a recognised clinical concern
\citep{mitchell2015}; the model's binary sites are not calibrated to it.
\item \emph{The earlier pooled metric.} The 17 October manuscript reported
``site pickup with no intervening opportunity'' (own-zone 0 plus carry-over 0):
57.8\% of independent-profile control acquisitions and 71.0\% of policy
acquisitions in the original workflow. That metric mixes two labels that behaved
very differently; the whole rise in its share came from carry-over
(18.9\%$\to$41.4\%), while the current-stay share fell. It is not a
hygiene-insensitive share. Likewise the earlier surface-label fractions of
control acquisitions (72.3\%, 39.6\% and 37.6\% in the three profiles) equal
own-zone plus other-zone plus carry-over in Table~\ref{tab:routes-orig}.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Caveats.} The labels describe realized paths, not causal route
fractions or attributable shares. In the original workflow a patient swapped
into a carrier's former bed was not admitted to that bed, so pickups of the
carrier's contamination are labelled own-zone current stay, not carry-over. Under
the alternative rules any remaining carry-over must have been deposited while
the bed stood empty, because moving in does not trigger cleaning. The original
and alternative counts come from different $p$, different seeds and different
studies; no between-workflow difference here has a declared estimand or
interval, and the design cannot attribute any difference to vacancy cleaning or
to the isolation rule. Why carry-over mattered most for independent misses is an
untested hypothesis: that profile needs a larger $p$ to reach the target (.143
against about .076), and a route through an initially clean site needs four
transmission events rather than two, so its weight relative to hand bridges
scales roughly with $p^2$, about 3.5 times larger for the independent profile at
the original probabilities.
