\emph{Face-validity and resource quantities (post hoc, round 1).}
Table~\ref{tab:face} reports descriptive quantities from the alternative-workflow
control arms. The mean fraction of contaminated bedside sites differs about
threefold between profiles at similar baselines (3.7\% against 1.3\% and 1.2\%),
so environmental or hand positivity could constrain $p$ separately from the
hygiene law; 2.1\% of ICU environmental samples were positive in one study
\citep{wei2021}, which is face validity only (organisms, sampling and binary
sites differ). The policy cost about 17,000 extra completed actions per run in
every profile, but the return per acquisition prevented differed threefold. In
these records, survival among naturally occurring contamination episodes
recovers the stationary $q_1$ and $q_2$, because contamination never changes
behaviour and pickups are independent of the completion state; selection of
natural episodes is therefore a real-world risk, not a property of the model.
